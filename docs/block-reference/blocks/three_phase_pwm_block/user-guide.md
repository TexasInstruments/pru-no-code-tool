# Three-phase complementary PWM (`three_phase_pwm_block`) — User Guide

> Generated by `build_block_docs.js`.

## What it does

Emit one synchronized three-phase complementary PWM period from SVPWM duties.

Consumes phase A/B/C duty counts and emits one edge-aligned PWM period on six R30 pins. Each call sorts its edges once into an event list (the duties are the same for every period of the call); each period then reads the IEP counter once per poll iteration, compares it with the next event time and writes R30 when that time is reached. Edge times in IEP counts from the period start: high sides on at DEADTIME, high-off at min(duty, PERIOD) - DEADTIME, low-on at duty + DEADTIME, the sample strobe at PERIOD/2, all off at PERIOD. A high side stays off when its duty is at or below 2 x DEADTIME (its on-time, duty - 2 x DEADTIME, would be zero or less); a low side stays off when duty >= PERIOD - DEADTIME, and the low side of a leg at duty 0 turns on with the high sides; duties above the period are limited to the period. Dead time is never a busy-wait: it exists only as event times. Nominally both devices of a half bridge are off for 2 x DEADTIME counts, from the ideal high-off (duty - DEADTIME) to the ideal low-on (duty + DEADTIME). The delivered gap can be shorter when a late high-off bunches with an on-time low-on; the smallest gaps observed are about 8 counts at DEADTIME 10 and 16 at DEADTIME 20 (for example duties 424, 424, 444 at PERIOD 800, and 4, 4, 44 at PERIOD 400 with DEADTIME 20). This minimum is an observed value pinned by tests/test_three_phase_scheduling.py, not a guarantee; the two devices are never on together. Edges with the same count change in one R30 write. Timing in cycles (= IEP counts): one poll iteration W = 4 (lbco of CNT + qbgt), counter sample to the R30 write X = 5, write to the next write when that edge is already due X2 = 8. An edge is never early; an isolated edge is late by at most W + X = 9 counts, and the k-th edge of a cluster of single-edge writes by at most W + X + (k-1) x X2. A write that changes several pins at once (coincident edges, clamped legs) goes through an escape token and takes up to 32 cycles to dispatch; the per-kind costs are in tests/test_three_phase_scheduling.py. The strobe rises at PERIOD/2 under the same bound and is one poll iteration (4 cycles) wide. DEADTIME is an 8-bit immediate (at most 255). The macro owns IEP GLOBAL_CFG, CMP_CFG, CMP0, CNT and CMP_STATUS for the period (CMP0 flags the period end without resetting the counter, so a late edge is never lost), then disables CMP0 before returning. By default one call emits one period. Set Period count to N (1..2147483647) to emit N periods in one call: each period restarts the IEP counter and is the same waveform, edge for edge, as one single-period call, and compare events are disabled after the last. Consecutive periods are separated by 32 cycles (a back-to-back pair of single-period calls is separated by 182 because each call sorts its edges again and re-programs the IEP); the final return costs 2 cycles more than a single-period call (period decrement and branch), all after the last all-off edge. At small PERIOD with duties near PERIOD the late edges can land after the nominal period end (PERIOD 64, DEADTIME 1: an edge up to 77 counts late, an effective period of about 126 counts). The generated macro is about 985 instructions per inlined instance, roughly a third of a 3072-instruction instruction RAM; the single-period and the counted macro are separate bodies. Conservative instruction bound scales with N.

Timing restrictions: this is a software block and its timing is not hardware-exact. (1) Dead time is nominally 2 x DEADTIME. The delivered minimum measured is about 8 counts at DEADTIME 10 and about 16 at DEADTIME 20, when a late high-off clusters with an on-time low-on. No overlap was seen in about 2000 configurations. It is an observed value pinned by tests, not a guaranteed minimum. (2) Single-edge writes are up to 8 counts late (W + X = 9 bound). Edges written together (coincident edges, clamped legs) are about 10 to 36 counts late in the test cases, against the 12-count budget of the single-edge path; example: PERIOD 800, DEADTIME 10, duties 400/400/400 gives low-ons 31 counts late. (3) This applies to the software block. ICSS-M on AM261x has no PWM module; on AM243x/AM64x the ICSSG hardware PWM module (not covered by this block) is the better fit when dead time must be a guaranteed minimum.

## How to use

1. In SysConfig (PRU No-Code), add a **Three-phase complementary PWM** block to your PRU core's flow.
2. Set the parameters above; wire `prev`/`next` to sequence it, and any `input`/`output` data ports to neighbouring blocks.
3. Generate — the block emits its `m_three_phase_pwm_period` invocation into the PRU assembly.
